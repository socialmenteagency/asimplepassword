Now let a password manager do the remembering.

Why not use the same password everywhere? Sites get hacked and their passwords leak. Criminals then try the leaked ones on your email, your bank and everything else, so one leak opens every account.

The fix is a different password for every site, and nobody can remember that many. You don’t have to. A password manager makes them, keeps them and types them for you. You remember just one.

Want to know if your email has already turned up in a leak? Check it at haveibeenpwned.com

  1. Remember one password.

    Turn on “Make it memorable”, set “Words” to 4 or 5 and learn it by heart. It opens your manager (or your Apple or Google account), so never use it anywhere else.

  2. Keep a paper copy.

    Write it on paper and keep copies in two safe places, like a home safe and a relative’s house or a bank box. Not in a phone note. If you forget it, most managers can’t reset it for you.

  3. Let it do the rest.

    For every other site, the manager creates a long random password, saves it and fills it in each time you log in. You never see it, type it or remember it.

Made on your device. Sent nowhere.

Each password is put together in your browser with its built-in secure random generator. There’s no server behind it, no account and no log of your passwords. Close the tab and it’s gone. The one exception is the endpoint for AI agents: see the last question below.

Why three words by default?

A password like pareja-VENDE-grito-6 is easy to read, remember and type on a phone, and much harder to guess than the ones people usually invent, like John131824 or Iamhappy2023. Those follow patterns every attacker tries first: names, dates, common phrases. Ours are picked at random from around 58 billion possibilities, with nothing about you in them.

Questions

Is it strong enough for my email or bank?

For most accounts, yes: it’s much harder to guess than a typical password. For your email or bank, add a word: set “Words” to 4 or 5. Want to check? Try the strength testers above. Better still, keep it in a password manager and use a different password on every site.

The site I’m signing up for won’t accept a dash.

Type the character it does accept (_ or . or anything else) in “Between words”, or choose none.

Why did my clipboard change?

Your first tap or key press on this page copies the password on screen, so it’s ready to paste. Every new password is copied too.

Do you store my passwords?

No. Nothing is saved except the language you pick, and only in this browser. Every visit starts with the default options. We count visits from the server’s standard logs, with no cookies and no trackers.

Can I check how it works?

Yes. The code is public on GitHub, so anyone can check it: github.com/socialmenteagency/asimplepassword

What does “Make it memorable” do?

It builds the password as a tiny sentence you can picture, like couple-SELLS-scream-6: a couple selling a scream in a jar. A picture is easier to remember than a string of letters. It’s a bit easier to guess than three random words, so for your email or bank, add a word.

Can my AI agent get a password here?

Yes, this site is built for that. An agent with a browser can call the page’s generate_password tool, and one that can only fetch a URL can use asimplepassword.com/api/password (options: words, sep, number, max, memorable, lang, format). Unlike the generator on this page, that endpoint makes the password on our server: nothing is stored or logged. For your own bank or email, make the password on this page instead, so no AI ever sees it.
